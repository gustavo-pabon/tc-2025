At Netflix, we aspire to entertain the world — and to do so sustainably and responsibly. You can read the full details of how in our fourth Environmental, Social and Corporate Governance (ESG) Report published today. But we wanted to highlight three areas upfront:

   Our business model is well proven and Netflix is a leader in streaming entertainment in terms of engagement, revenue and profit. So we’re evolving to a more standard large-cap governance structure, including the phased declassification of our board, the elimination of supermajority voting provisions and changes to our executive compensation program from 2023 onwards.

Over the last 25 years, we’ve worked hard to create long-term value for our stakeholders — in particular members, shareholders and the creative community. While we’ve made good progress, there’s still more to do. # Sustainability in Our Operations and Value Chain

## Climate Transition Plan

To meet these objectives, Netflix is focused on **four key levers**:

1. **Energy Efficiency**  
   - **Description**: Identifying cost-effective efficiency improvements for energy used in offices and studios.  
   - **Icon**: A stylized energy efficiency symbol (lightning bolt within a square).

   - **Icon**: A car with a lightning bolt symbol.

3. **Clean Mobile Power**  
   - **Description**: Using alternatives to diesel generators on productions.  
   - **Icon**: A lightning bolt symbol with a mobile power icon.

4. **Renewable Energy**  
   - **Description**: Using renewable electricity and fuels.  
   - **Icon**: A circular arrow surrounding a lightning bolt.

---

## OED Framework

Netflix approaches these four levers using an **OED framework**—a structured methodology for optimizing energy use, electrifying operations, and decarbonizing the rest.

### Key Components of the OED Framework:

### Corporate Governance Best Practices and Stockholder Rights

1. **One share, one vote**:  
   We have a single class of shares with each share entitled to one vote.

2. **Majority voting standard**:  
   We have a majority voting standard in uncontested director elections. Any incumbent director who fails to receive a majority of votes cast in an uncontested election must tender their resignation to the board. The Nominating and Governance Committee would then make a recommendation to the board about whether to accept or reject the resignation or take other action.

3. **Annual director elections (fully declassified by 2025)**:  
   We have phased-in the declassification of our board with directors elected at this year’s annual meeting serving one-year terms and the entire board standing for annual elections beginning in 2025 and beyond.

4. **Elimination of supermajority voting**:  
   We eliminated supermajority voting provisions in our Charter and Bylaws.

5. **Proxy Access**:  
   A group of up to 20 stockholders, owning at least 3% of shares continuously for at least three years may nominate up to two directors or 20% of the Board (whichever is greater) for inclusion in our proxy statement.

6. **Stockholder right to call a special meeting**:  
   Stockholders holding a net-long position of not less than 20% in the Company continuously for at least one year may call a special meeting.

### Greenhouse Gas Emissions (GHG) Inventory Methodology
Netflix reports its emissions following the World Resources Institute (WRI) / World Business Council for Sustainable Development’s (WBCSD) Greenhouse Gas Protocol Corporate Standard, as amended by the GHG Protocol Scope 2 Guidance, as well as WRI / WBCSD’s Greenhouse Gas Protocol Corporate Value Chain (Scope 3) Accounting and Reporting Standard and Technical Guidance for Calculating Scope 3 Emissions (collectively, the GHG Protocol). Netflix GHG emissions reporting follows the operational control approach set out by the GHGP.

Our reported emissions account for all GHGs covered by the UNFCCC Kyoto Protocol (Annex A) relevant to Netflix activities and are converted into metric tons of carbon dioxide equivalents (MTCO2e) as specified by the GHG Protocol. All emission factors are applied to the data and updated annually to reflect the latest guidance and factors published by US EPA Emission Factors for Greenhouse Gas Inventories (2022), US EPA eGRID2021 Electricity Grid Emission Factors (2023) and UK DEFRA Greenhouse Gas Reporting Conversion Factors (2022). Additionally, where data is not available or of sufficient quality, Netflix uses proxy data, industry-average figures, or expert assumptions. In such instances, we use third-party sources for reliability and completeness.

Some of these data sets such as the average building energy use intensity values from the DOE Commercial Building Energy Consumption Survey (2016), are held constant to maintain consistency across years.

Netflix’s greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions reporting is consistent with the “operational control” approach as set out by the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard: Revised Edition. The organizational and operational boundary applies to the global company including its subsidiaries, the office and studio facilities we own and operate (e.g., Albuquerque Studios) as well as facilities we lease from others but over which we have meaningful operational control (e.g., corporate and studio offices and stages).

### Our Footprint Boundary

#### Scope 1 and 2:
Our emissions include all Scope 1 (direct) and Scope 2 (indirect emissions) from the following:
- **Corporate operations**: Offices, etc.
- **Production**: Films, series, and games.
- **Storage and delivery**: Content storage and delivery.

Scope 2 emissions are calculated using market-based and location-based emissions accounting methods defined by the GHG Protocol Scope 2 Guidance:
- **Location-based estimates**: Calculated based on the emissions intensity of the locations where electricity consumption occurs.
- **Market-based method**: Incorporates electricity procurement decisions chosen within the local electricity market, including:
  - Zero-carbon electricity supply from utilities (e.g., opt-in “green tariff” rates).
